How to Read This Book

Every chapter follows the same shape. Five learning objectives state what you should be able to do afterwards, and the last one is always to implement something. A short orienting paragraph says why the chapter exists. Numbered sections carry the argument, interleaved with boxed definitions, theorems (with proofs or proof sketches), worked numeric examples small enough to check by hand, and Common Pitfall boxes naming real implementation bugs. Each chapter ends with exactly one Artifact: a complete, runnable module that builds the chapter's core idea from scratch and verifies itself against a reference. The code is not illustration. It is evidence.

Claims carry confidence tags. Certain means textbook-stable. Likely means a strong inference the literature supports but has not settled. Verify means correct as of writing but attached to a moving API or a number that will drift — check Appendix F before you cite it. Contested means the field genuinely disagrees; those passages present both positions and state which evidence would change the verdict. A book about a field moving this fast that used only one tag would be lying.

A recurring Under Encryption box appears wherever a construct has a consequence for computing on ciphertext. Homomorphic encryption evaluates polynomials cheaply and everything else expensively, so a surprising amount of ordinary architectural choice — the softmax, the normalization layer, the activation function, the very decision to quantize — is load-bearing the moment you encrypt. Those boxes are pointers into Part VIII, which treats the subject at research level. Readers with no interest in encrypted computation can skip every one of them without losing the thread.

Appendix E maps each chapter onto the corresponding treatment in Géron, Raschka, Trask, Serrano, and Chakraborty, and every chapter carries an inline pointer to its counterparts. Read the fundamentals in at least two of them. One book's explanation of backpropagation is a claim; two independent explanations that agree are closer to understanding.
