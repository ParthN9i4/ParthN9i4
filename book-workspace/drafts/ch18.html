Attention is content-addressable soft retrieval: every position emits a query, every position advertises a key, the match scores become a probability distribution, and the output is the corresponding convex combination of values. That is the entire mechanism. The transformer is what you get when you make retrieval the only operation that moves information between positions, give up the recurrence of Chapter 17 along with its sequential bottleneck, and agree to pay $O(n^2)$ for the privilege. This chapter derives the mechanism (18.2), its backward pass, and the one free parameter in it — the $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ temperature, which exists for exactly one reason. Then the engineering: masking and its most common bug (18.4), the full block and its parameter count (18.5), the KV-cache economics that drive multi-query and grouped-query attention (18.6), and the quadratic wall (18.7). Chapter 19 adds positional information, which the mechanism derived here conspicuously lacks; Chapter 31 makes it fast.

18.1 From seq2seq Attention to Self-Attention

Attention was invented to fix a specific defect. An encoder–decoder RNN compresses the entire source sentence into one fixed-width state $h_n$, and the decoder reads only that vector; translation quality collapses as source length grows because a 64-dimensional state cannot hold a 40-word sentence. Bahdanau's fix was to let the decoder read all encoder states, weighted by a learned relevance score,

$$e_{ij} = v^{\top}\tanh(W_s s_{i-1} + W_h h_j), \qquad \alpha_{ij} = \frac{\exp e_{ij}}{\sum_{j'} \exp e_{ij'}}, \qquad c_i = \sum_j \alpha_{ij} h_j .$$

Luong replaced the additive MLP score with $e_{ij} = s_{i-1}^{\top} h_j$, which is cheaper because it is a matrix multiplication rather than an elementwise nonlinearity over $n$ pairs. Both were bolted onto a recurrence that still ran sequentially.

Self-attention is the observation that the queries need not come from a decoder at all: let a sequence attend to itself, and the recurrence has nothing left to do. The consequence is a different complexity profile, not merely a different architecture.

Layer typeCompute per layerSequential stepsMax path length
Recurrent$O(n d^2)$$O(n)$$O(n)$
Convolutional, kernel $k$$O(k n d^2)$$O(1)$$O(\log_k n)$
Self-attention$O(n^2 d)$$O(1)$$O(1)$

Two columns matter. Sequential steps is why transformers train on modern hardware and LSTMs do not: an RNN's $O(n)$ dependent steps cannot be filled with parallel work, so a GPU runs at a few percent of peak. Max path length is the gradient argument from Chapter 17 restated — any two positions are one hop apart, so the multiplicative chain that destroys long-range RNN gradients has length one. The price is the first column: attention is cheaper than recurrence only while $n < d$, and Section 18.7 makes that trade exact. Certain

18.2 Queries, Keys, Values

Definition 18.1 (Scaled dot-product attention)

Given queries $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n_q \times d_k}$, keys $K \in \mathbb{R}^{n_k \times d_k}$ and values $V \in \mathbb{R}^{n_k \times d_v}$, stored one item per row, $$\mathrm{Attn}(Q,K,V) \;=\; \underbrace{\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}} + M\right)}_{A \,\in\, \mathbb{R}^{n_q \times n_k}} V ,$$ with the softmax taken over each row and $M$ an additive mask with entries in $\{0, -\infty\}$. Each row of $A$ is a probability vector, so each output row is a convex combination of value rows: attention cannot extrapolate outside the convex hull of $V$.

Definition 18.2 (Self-attention)

For a token matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ and weight matrices $W_q, W_k, W_v \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k \times d}$ acting on column vectors, self-attention sets $Q = XW_q^{\top}$, $K = XW_k^{\top}$, $V = XW_v^{\top}$. Queries, keys and values are three linear views of the same sequence; nothing else in the layer depends on position, so self-attention is exactly permutation-equivariant — permuting the rows of $X$ permutes the rows of the output identically. Positional information must be injected from outside, which is Chapter 19's subject.

Backward pass. Let $\bar{Z} \equiv \partial\mathcal{L}/\partial Z$. From $O = AV$ we get $\bar{V} = A^{\top}\bar{O}$ and $\bar{A} = \bar{O}V^{\top}$ immediately. The softmax is the only interesting step.

Theorem 18.1 (Softmax Jacobian and its contraction)

For $p = \mathrm{softmax}(s)$ with $s \in \mathbb{R}^{m}$, $\;\partial p_i/\partial s_j = p_i(\delta_{ij} - p_j)$, i.e. $J = \mathrm{diag}(p) - pp^{\top}$. Consequently the vector–Jacobian product needed by backpropagation is $$\bar{s} \;=\; J^{\top}\bar{p} \;=\; p \odot \big(\bar{p} - \langle \bar{p}, p\rangle \mathbf{1}\big),$$ computable in $O(m)$ without forming $J$.

Proof. Write $p_i = e^{s_i}/Z$ with $Z = \sum_l e^{s_l}$. Then $\partial p_i/\partial s_j = (\delta_{ij}e^{s_i}Z - e^{s_i}e^{s_j})/Z^2 = p_i\delta_{ij} - p_ip_j$. $J$ is symmetric, so $\bar{s}_j = \sum_i \bar{p}_i p_i(\delta_{ij}-p_j) = p_j\bar{p}_j - p_j\sum_i \bar{p}_ip_i$. $\blacksquare$

Applying Theorem 18.1 row-wise to $\bar{A}$ gives $\bar{S}$, and the rest is bilinear:

$$\bar{S} = A \odot \big(\bar{A} - \mathrm{rowsum}(\bar{A}\odot A)\mathbf{1}^{\top}\big)\big/\sqrt{d_k},\qquad \bar{Q} = \bar{S}K,\qquad \bar{K} = \bar{S}^{\top}Q,$$ $$\bar{W}_q = \bar{Q}^{\top}X,\qquad \bar{X} \;\mathrel{+}=\; \bar{Q}W_q \;+\; \bar{K}W_k \;+\; \bar{V}W_v .$$

Note that $\bar{Q}$ and $\bar{K}$ are transposes of each other's construction: the score matrix is bilinear in $Q$ and $K$, so the backward pass through it is a matched pair of matrix products. Note also what must be kept alive between forward and backward: $A$ itself, $n \times n$ per head. That $O(n^2)$ activation, not the FLOPs, is what made long context infeasible before FlashAttention recomputed $A$ tile-by-tile in the backward pass (Chapter 31). Certain

18.2.1 Why the Scaling Factor
Theorem 18.2 (Logit variance under standard initialization)

Let $q, k \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k}$ have mutually independent entries with mean $0$ and variance $1$. Then $\mathbb{E}[q^{\top}k] = 0$ and $\mathrm{Var}(q^{\top}k) = d_k$; hence $\mathrm{Var}\big(q^{\top}k/\sqrt{d_k}\big) = 1$.

Proof. $\mathbb{E}[q^{\top}k] = \sum_i \mathbb{E}[q_i]\mathbb{E}[k_i] = 0$. For the second moment, $\mathbb{E}[(q^{\top}k)^2] = \sum_{i,j}\mathbb{E}[q_iq_jk_ik_j]$. For $i \neq j$ independence factorizes the term into $\mathbb{E}[q_i]\mathbb{E}[q_j]\mathbb{E}[k_i]\mathbb{E}[k_j] = 0$; the $i = j$ terms give $\mathbb{E}[q_i^2]\mathbb{E}[k_i^2] = 1$ each. Summing, $\mathrm{Var} = d_k$. $\blacksquare$

The hypothesis is what standard initialization delivers: if $x$ has unit-variance coordinates and $W_q$ has i.i.d. entries of variance $1/d$ (Chapter 12), then $q = W_qx$ has coordinates of variance $1$, and likewise for $k$.

Without the divisor, logit standard deviation grows as $\sqrt{d_k}$ — a factor of 8 at the near-universal $d_k = 64$, and the gap between the largest and second-largest of $n$ such logits grows like $\sigma\sqrt{2\ln n}$. Softmax of large-spread logits is nearly one-hot, and by Theorem 18.1 a one-hot $p$ has a Jacobian of essentially zero: if $p_m = 1-\varepsilon$, then every entry of $J$ is $O(\varepsilon)$. The layer stops passing gradient before it has learned anything about which key to select. The scale factor is a temperature, and its only job is keeping softmax out of saturation at initialization. Certain

Example 18.1 (Saturation in four dimensions)

Take $d_k = 4$, $q = (1,1,1,1)$, $k_1 = (1,1,1,1)$, $k_2 = (1,1,-1,-1)$. Then $q^{\top}k_1 = 4$ and $q^{\top}k_2 = 0$.

Unscaled. $\mathrm{softmax}(4,0) = \left(\tfrac{e^4}{e^4+1}, \tfrac{1}{e^4+1}\right) = (0.9820,\,0.0180)$, since $e^4 = 54.598$.

Scaled by $1/\sqrt{4} = 1/2$. $\mathrm{softmax}(2,0) = \left(\tfrac{e^2}{e^2+1}, \tfrac{1}{e^2+1}\right) = (0.8808,\,0.1192)$, since $e^2 = 7.389$.

The off-diagonal Jacobian entry $-p_1p_2$ is $-0.0177$ unscaled and $-0.1050$ scaled: dividing by 2 multiplied the available gradient signal by about six. At the realistic $d_k = 64$ the artifact measures the same effect on random logits — the largest of 8 attention weights is $0.9998$ unscaled and $0.5119$ scaled.

One caveat, because it will matter in Chapter 15's setting: $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ is the correct initialization-time scale, and the independence hypothesis of Theorem 18.2 fails after training, when queries and keys become correlated. The muP prescription is therefore $1/d_k$ rather than $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ for attention logits when transferring hyperparameters across widths, and it demonstrably transfers better; standard transformers nevertheless ship $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ because that is what the pretrained checkpoints were trained with. Likely

18.3 Multi-Head Attention

Definition 18.3 (Multi-head attention)

With $H$ heads and $d_h = d/H$, take $W_q^{(h)}, W_k^{(h)}, W_v^{(h)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h \times d}$ and $W_o \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, and set $$\mathrm{MHA}(X) = \mathrm{Concat}\big(\mathrm{head}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{head}_H\big)W_o^{\top}, \qquad \mathrm{head}_h = \mathrm{Attn}\big(XW_q^{(h)\top}, XW_k^{(h)\top}, XW_v^{(h)\top}\big).$$ Splitting $W_o$ column-blockwise as $W_o = [W_o^{(1)} \cdots W_o^{(H)}]$ turns the concatenation into a sum, $\mathrm{MHA}(X) = \sum_h A_h X W_v^{(h)\top}W_o^{(h)\top}$.

The summed form is the useful one. Head $h$ contributes an additive term whose feature-space action is the single matrix $W_o^{(h)}W_v^{(h)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ of rank at most $d_h$, and whose position-space action is $A_h$, determined by $W_q^{(h)\top}W_k^{(h)}$, also of rank at most $d_h$. Only these two products are identifiable: any invertible $R$ with $W_q \to RW_q$, $W_k \to R^{-\top}W_k$ leaves the layer unchanged. Reading individual $W_q$ entries is therefore meaningless; reading the products is not. Likely

Because $d_h = d/H$, multi-head attention has exactly the parameter count of one-head attention at full width: $4d^2$ weights (plus $4d$ biases), independent of $H$. Heads buy not capacity but the ability to attend to several places at once — one head can track the syntactic subject while another tracks the previous token — which a single softmax, being one probability distribution per query, structurally cannot do. The cost is rank: at $H = 96$ and $d = 12288$, each head's OV circuit is rank $\leq 128$ out of $12288$. Certain

18.4 Masking and the Mask-Then-Softmax Ordering Bug

A decoder-only language model must not let position $i$ see position $j > i$, or the next-token objective becomes trivial. The causal mask $M_{ij} = 0$ for $j \leq i$ and $-\infty$ otherwise is added to the logits, so that $\exp(-\infty) = 0$ and the masked entries drop out of both numerator and denominator. Padding masks work identically, with an extra column pattern per sequence.

Common Pitfall

Multiplying the mask into the probabilities instead of adding it to the logits. Writing A = softmax(S) * mask rather than A = softmax(S + M) produces rows that sum to less than one, because the normalizer $Z$ still contains the future tokens' $e^{s_j}$. The output is then a shrunken, position-dependently scaled convex combination — and crucially it is still a smooth function of the parameters with a plausible-looking gradient, so the model trains, the loss goes down, and nothing crashes. It merely converges to a worse optimum, and its outputs diverge from any reference implementation. The artifact's torch cross-check catches this in one line; a training run will not.

Two companion bugs. First, using a large finite constant instead of $-\infty$: $-10^9$ overflows fp16's $65504$ maximum and becomes $-\infty$ or NaN on cast, while $-10^4$ is not large enough to suppress a logit of $10^3$. Use $-\infty$, or the dtype's most negative finite value. Second, an entirely masked row — which arises for padding query positions, not for causal masking, since the diagonal is always visible — makes softmax compute $0/0$ and emit NaN that propagates through the whole batch on the backward pass. Zero those output rows explicitly.

18.5 The Full Block: Residual Stream, Feed-Forward, Norms

A transformer block is two sublayers, each wrapped in a residual connection. In the modern pre-norm arrangement,

$$h = x + \mathrm{MHA}\big(\mathrm{LN}(x)\big), \qquad y = h + W_2\,\phi\big(W_1\,\mathrm{LN}(h) + c_1\big) + c_2,$$

with $W_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{ff}} \times d}$, $d_{\text{ff}} = 4d$ conventionally, and $\phi$ a GELU or a gated variant. The original post-norm form, $x \leftarrow \mathrm{LN}(x + \mathrm{Sublayer}(x))$, puts a normalization on the residual path; the gradient must then pass through every LayerNorm on its way back, and training a deep post-norm stack requires a learning-rate warmup to avoid divergence in the first few hundred steps. Pre-norm leaves an unobstructed identity path from the loss to every layer's input, needs no warmup to remain stable, and is what essentially every model since GPT-2 uses. Its cost is that the residual stream's variance grows roughly linearly with depth, since each block adds a roughly independent increment, so a final LayerNorm before the output head is mandatory. Certain

Parameter count, exactly, with $d_{\text{ff}} = 4d$: attention contributes $4d^2 + 4d$, the FFN $8d^2 + 5d$, and the two LayerNorms $4d$, for $12d^2 + 13d$ per block — the $12d^2$ per layer that Chapter 24's scaling laws take as given. For GPT-2 small ($d = 768$, $L = 12$, vocabulary $50257$, context $1024$): $7{,}087{,}872$ parameters per block, $85{,}054{,}464$ across 12 blocks, plus $38{,}597{,}376$ token embeddings, $786{,}432$ position embeddings and $1{,}536$ for the final LayerNorm, totalling $124{,}439{,}808$ — the familiar 124M, with the output head tied to the embedding matrix. Certain

18.6 MQA, GQA, and KV-Cache Economics

During autoregressive decoding, each new token's keys and values are appended to a cache so that previously computed ones are never recomputed. The cache holds

$$\text{bytes} \;=\; 2\,L\,n\,H_{kv}\,d_h\,b ,$$

two tensors per layer, $L$ layers, $n$ positions, $H_{kv}$ key/value heads of width $d_h$, at $b$ bytes per element. This is per sequence, and it scales with batch size, which is what makes it the binding constraint on serving throughput (Chapter 33). For $d = 4096$, $H = 32$, $L = 32$, $n = 8192$, fp16, the artifact computes $4.000$ GiB — comparable to a quarter of the 13B-class model's own weights, for a single conversation.

Multi-query attention (Shazeer) keeps $H$ query heads but shares one key/value head, $H_{kv} = 1$, cutting the cache by $32\times$ to $0.125$ GiB in that configuration. Grouped-query attention interpolates: $H_{kv}$ groups, each shared by $H/H_{kv}$ query heads, so $H_{kv} = 8$ gives $1.000$ GiB at $4\times$ reduction. The reason the reduction matters more than the FLOP saving is arithmetic intensity: in the decode step each cached KV element is read once and used in a handful of multiply–adds, so the attention step is purely memory-bandwidth-bound and its wall-clock is proportional to cache bytes moved, not FLOPs (Chapter 30).

The empirical claim — that GQA at $H_{kv} = 8$ recovers essentially all of MHA's quality while MQA at $H_{kv} = 1$ measurably loses some, and that an existing MHA checkpoint can be converted by mean-pooling each group's KV projections and uptraining on a few percent of the original tokens — comes from Ainslie et al. (2023) and has been reproduced at scale by the Llama-2/3 and Mistral families. Whether the loss at $H_{kv}=1$ is intrinsic or a training-budget artifact is not settled; DeepSeek's multi-head latent attention, which caches a low-rank joint projection instead of reducing head count, reports beating MHA quality at a smaller cache than GQA, which would suggest the head-count axis was never the right one. Contested The experiment that settles it is a matched-compute sweep of $H_{kv}$ and latent rank at fixed cache bytes; published comparisons vary too many things at once.

18.7 Complexity Accounting and the Quadratic Wall

Count a multiply–add as 2 FLOPs. One block's forward pass over $n$ tokens costs

$$\underbrace{8nd^2}_{Q,K,V,O \text{ projections}} \;+\; \underbrace{4n\,d\,d_{\text{ff}}}_{\text{FFN}} \;+\; \underbrace{4n^2d}_{QK^{\top} \text{ and } AV} .$$

The quadratic term overtakes the FFN term when $4n^2d = 4nd\,d_{\text{ff}}$, i.e. at

$$n^{*} = d_{\text{ff}} = 4d \quad(\text{conventional expansion}),$$

and it overtakes all three linear terms combined at $n = 2d + d_{\text{ff}} = 6d$. This is the crossover stated in terms of model width: attention is a minor cost below a sequence length of about four times the residual width and the dominant cost above it. For $d = 768$ that is $n^{*} = 3072$; for $d = 12288$ it is $49152$. Equivalently, the standard training-compute estimate $C \approx 6ND$ (Chapter 24), which counts only the linear terms, is short by a factor $1 + n/(6d)$ — under 3% for GPT-3's $n=2048$, $d=12288$, but a factor of $6.3$ for a 128K context at $d = 4096$. Certain

Two honest qualifications. FLOPs are not time: the two attention matmuls have far lower arithmetic intensity than the projections, and before FlashAttention the real limit was materializing the $n \times n$ matrix in HBM, not multiplying by it. And the wall is quadratic in compute, not memory, once attention is tiled — which is why 1M-token contexts exist at all despite $4n^2d$ being astronomically large.

18.8 The Residual Stream as a Communication Channel

Reading the pre-norm block as $x^{(l+1)} = x^{(l)} + (\text{increment})$ makes the residual stream the object of interest: a $d$-dimensional bus that every layer reads from and adds to, and the only channel between layers. Anything layer 20 needs from layer 3 was written into that bus and not overwritten — there is no other path. Two consequences follow. First, the bus has finite bandwidth: $d$ dimensions shared by all $L$ layers' worth of features, so features must either occupy near-orthogonal subspaces or tolerate interference, and the number of features a network represents far exceeds $d$ (Chapter 37's superposition). Second, the division of labour is clean: attention is the only operation that moves information between positions, and the FFN is the only one that transforms information within a position. Every subsequent architecture in Part IV — state space models included (Chapter 21) — keeps this factorization and replaces only the mixer. Likely

Artifact 18.1 — Multi-head attention and a pre-norm transformer block in NumPy

A complete block from scratch, with four independent verifications: the forward pass against torch.nn.MultiheadAttention with transplanted weights, the analytic backward pass against central finite differences on every parameter tensor and on the input, and token-at-a-time KV-cached decoding against full recomputation — the assertion that catches real cache bugs, since an off-by-one in the mask offset or a stale cache still produces plausible text. It closes with the FLOP and cache tables of Sections 18.6 and 18.7.

"""
Artifact 18.1 -- Multi-head attention and a pre-norm transformer block, from scratch.
Verifies: (1) logit variance is d_k unscaled and ~1 after dividing by sqrt(d_k);
(2) NumPy MHA forward == torch.nn.MultiheadAttention with transplanted weights;
(3) analytic backward (incl. softmax Jacobian) == central finite differences;
(4) incremental KV-cached decoding == full recomputation;
(5) FLOP and KV-cache tables locating where quadratic attention overtakes the FFN.
Pure NumPy core; torch is a guarded cross-check only.
"""
import numpy as np
try:
    import torch
    import torch.nn as nn
except ImportError:                      # the artifact must run without torch
    torch = None

# --------------------------------------------------------------------- primitives

def softmax(z, axis=-1):
    """Max-subtracted softmax: the shift is exact (softmax is shift-invariant) and
    is the only thing keeping exp() out of overflow. See Chapter 5."""
    e = np.exp(z - np.max(z, axis=axis, keepdims=True))
    return e / np.sum(e, axis=axis, keepdims=True)

def softmax_backward(dA, A, axis=-1):
    """JVP for p = softmax(s): ds = p * (dp - <dp, p>). This is J = diag(p) - p p^T
    contracted with dp, done without ever forming the T x T Jacobian."""
    return A * (dA - np.sum(dA * A, axis=axis, keepdims=True))

def gelu(x):
    c = np.sqrt(2.0 / np.pi)
    return 0.5 * x * (1.0 + np.tanh(c * (x + 0.044715 * x ** 3)))

def gelu_grad(x):
    c = np.sqrt(2.0 / np.pi)
    t = np.tanh(c * (x + 0.044715 * x ** 3))
    return 0.5 * (1 + t) + 0.5 * x * (1 - t * t) * c * (1 + 3 * 0.044715 * x ** 2)

def layernorm(x, g, b, eps=1e-5):
    inv = 1.0 / np.sqrt(x.var(-1, keepdims=True) + eps)
    xhat = (x - x.mean(-1, keepdims=True)) * inv
    return g * xhat + b, (xhat, inv, g)

def layernorm_backward(dy, cache):
    xhat, inv, g = cache
    d = xhat.shape[-1]
    dg = (dy * xhat).reshape(-1, d).sum(0)
    db = dy.reshape(-1, d).sum(0)
    dxhat = dy * g
    dx = inv * (dxhat - dxhat.mean(-1, keepdims=True)
                - xhat * (dxhat * xhat).mean(-1, keepdims=True))
    return dx, dg, db

# --------------------------------------------------------------------- attention

class MHA:
    """Weights act as y = W x on column vectors, so row-of-tokens code is X @ W.T."""

    def __init__(self, d, H, rng):
        self.d, self.H, self.dh = d, H, d // H
        s = d ** -0.5
        self.p = dict(Wq=rng.normal(0, s, (d, d)), Wk=rng.normal(0, s, (d, d)),
                      Wv=rng.normal(0, s, (d, d)), Wo=rng.normal(0, s, (d, d)),
                      bq=rng.normal(0, .1, d), bk=rng.normal(0, .1, d),
                      bv=rng.normal(0, .1, d), bo=rng.normal(0, .1, d))

    def _split(self, Z):                 # (T,d) -> (H,T,dh)
        return Z.reshape(Z.shape[0], self.H, self.dh).transpose(1, 0, 2)

    def _merge(self, Z):                 # (H,T,dh) -> (T,d)
        return Z.transpose(1, 0, 2).reshape(-1, self.d)

    def forward(self, X, causal=True, cache=None, offset=0):
        """cache=None: full forward. cache={'K':...,'V':...}: append this step's K,V
        and attend over everything stored. offset = absolute index of X[0]."""
        p = self.p
        Q = self._split(X @ p["Wq"].T + p["bq"])
        K = self._split(X @ p["Wk"].T + p["bk"])
        V = self._split(X @ p["Wv"].T + p["bv"])
        if cache is not None:
            if cache["K"] is not None:
                K = np.concatenate([cache["K"], K], axis=1)
                V = np.concatenate([cache["V"], V], axis=1)
            cache["K"], cache["V"] = K, V
        S = (Q @ K.transpose(0, 2, 1)) / np.sqrt(self.dh)        # (H,Tq,Tk)
        if causal:
            i = np.arange(S.shape[1])[:, None] + offset
            j = np.arange(S.shape[2])[None, :]
            # MASK THEN SOFTMAX: additive -inf on logits, never a multiply on the
            # probabilities. Zeroing after softmax leaves the denominator wrong.
            S = np.where(j <= i, S, -np.inf)
        A = softmax(S)
        Ctx = self._merge(A @ V)
        self.cache = (X, Q, K, V, A, Ctx)
        return Ctx @ p["Wo"].T + p["bo"]

    def backward(self, dO):
        X, Q, K, V, A, Ctx = self.cache
        p, g = self.p, {}
        g["Wo"], g["bo"] = dO.T @ Ctx, dO.sum(0)
        dCtx = self._split(dO @ p["Wo"])
        dA = dCtx @ V.transpose(0, 2, 1)
        dV = A.transpose(0, 2, 1) @ dCtx
        dS = softmax_backward(dA, A) / np.sqrt(self.dh)
        dQ, dK = dS @ K, dS.transpose(0, 2, 1) @ Q
        dX = np.zeros_like(X)
        for nm, dZ in (("q", self._merge(dQ)), ("k", self._merge(dK)),
                       ("v", self._merge(dV))):
            g["W" + nm], g["b" + nm] = dZ.T @ X, dZ.sum(0)
            dX = dX + dZ @ p["W" + nm]
        return dX, g

class Block:
    """Pre-norm: h = x + Attn(LN(x)); y = h + FFN(LN(h)). The residual stream is never
    normalized in place -- each sublayer reads a normalized copy of it and writes back
    an unnormalized increment."""

    def __init__(self, d, H, dff, rng):
        self.attn = MHA(d, H, rng)
        self.p = dict(g1=np.ones(d), b1=np.zeros(d), g2=np.ones(d), b2=np.zeros(d),
                      W1=rng.normal(0, d ** -0.5, (dff, d)), c1=np.zeros(dff),
                      W2=rng.normal(0, dff ** -0.5, (d, dff)), c2=np.zeros(d))

    def forward(self, X, cache=None, offset=0):
        p = self.p
        n1, c1 = layernorm(X, p["g1"], p["b1"])
        h = X + self.attn.forward(n1, cache=cache, offset=offset)
        n2, c2 = layernorm(h, p["g2"], p["b2"])
        u = n2 @ p["W1"].T + p["c1"]
        a = gelu(u)
        self.cache = (c1, c2, n2, u, a)
        return h + a @ p["W2"].T + p["c2"]

    def backward(self, dY):
        p, (c1, c2, n2, u, a) = self.p, self.cache
        g = {"W2": dY.T @ a, "c2": dY.sum(0)}
        du = (dY @ p["W2"]) * gelu_grad(u)
        g["W1"], g["c1"] = du.T @ n2, du.sum(0)
        dh, g["g2"], g["b2"] = layernorm_backward(du @ p["W1"], c2)
        dh = dh + dY                                   # residual around the FFN
        dn1, ga = self.attn.backward(dh)
        dX, g["g1"], g["b1"] = layernorm_backward(dn1, c1)
        return dX + dh, {**g, **{"attn." + k: v for k, v in ga.items()}}

    def params(self):
        return {**self.p, **{"attn." + k: v for k, v in self.attn.p.items()}}

def check_scaling(rng, dk=64, n=200_000):
    q, k = rng.normal(size=(n, dk)), rng.normal(size=(n, dk))
    raw = (q * k).sum(1)
    print(f"  Var(q.k)          = {raw.var():8.3f}   (predicted d_k = {dk})")
    print(f"  Var(q.k/sqrt(dk)) = {(raw / np.sqrt(dk)).var():8.3f}   (predicted 1.000)")
    lo = softmax(raw[:8][None, :])[0].max()
    hi = softmax(raw[:8][None, :] / np.sqrt(dk))[0].max()
    print(f"  max prob over 8 keys: unscaled {lo:.4f}  scaled {hi:.4f}")

def check_torch(blk, X, d, H):
    if torch is None:
        print("  [skipped: torch not installed]")
        return
    m = nn.MultiheadAttention(d, H, batch_first=True, dtype=torch.float64)
    p, T = blk.attn.p, X.shape[0]
    with torch.no_grad():
        m.in_proj_weight.copy_(torch.tensor(np.concatenate([p["Wq"], p["Wk"], p["Wv"]])))
        m.in_proj_bias.copy_(torch.tensor(np.concatenate([p["bq"], p["bk"], p["bv"]])))
        m.out_proj.weight.copy_(torch.tensor(p["Wo"]))
        m.out_proj.bias.copy_(torch.tensor(p["bo"]))
        mask = torch.triu(torch.full((T, T), float("-inf"), dtype=torch.float64), 1)
        t = torch.tensor(X)[None]
        ref, _ = m(t, t, t, attn_mask=mask, need_weights=False)
    err = np.abs(blk.attn.forward(X) - ref[0].numpy()).max()
    print(f"  max|numpy - torch.nn.MultiheadAttention| = {err:.3e}")
    assert err < 1e-9, err

def check_backward(blk, X, G, rng, k=4, eps=1e-6):
    Y = blk.forward(X)
    dX, grads = blk.backward(G)
    def loss():
        return np.sum(blk.forward(X) * G)
    worst = 0.0
    targets = [(P, grads[nm]) for nm, P in blk.params().items()] + [(X, dX)]
    for P, gm in targets:
        for _ in range(k):
            ix = tuple(rng.integers(0, s) for s in P.shape)
            old = P[ix]
            P[ix] = old + eps; lp = loss()
            P[ix] = old - eps; lm = loss()
            P[ix] = old
            num = (lp - lm) / (2 * eps)
            worst = max(worst, abs(num - gm[ix]) / max(1.0, abs(num)))
    print(f"  worst relative grad error, {k} coords per tensor = {worst:.3e}")
    assert worst < 1e-6, worst
    return Y

def check_kv_cache(blk, X, Y_full):
    """The assertion that catches real cache bugs: token-at-a-time decoding must
    reproduce the full-sequence forward exactly, offset in the causal mask included."""
    cache, rows = {"K": None, "V": None}, []
    for t in range(X.shape[0]):
        rows.append(blk.forward(X[t:t + 1], cache=cache, offset=t)[0])
    err = np.abs(np.stack(rows) - Y_full).max()
    print(f"  max|cached decode - full recompute| = {err:.3e}")
    assert err < 1e-6, err

def flop_table(d=768, H=12, dff=None):
    dff = dff or 4 * d
    print(f"  d_model={d} heads={H} d_head={d//H} d_ff={dff}  (one block, fwd, 2 FLOP/MAC)")
    print(f"  {'n':>6} {'QKVO 8nd^2':>14} {'FFN 4n d dff':>15} {'attn 4n^2 d':>15} {'ratio':>7}")
    for n in (128, 512, 1024, 2048, 3072, 4096, 8192, 16384):
        qkvo, ffn, att = 8 * n * d * d, 4 * n * d * dff, 4 * n * n * d
        print(f"  {n:>6} {qkvo:>14,} {ffn:>15,} {att:>15,} {att/ffn:>7.3f}")
    print(f"  attn == FFN at n = d_ff = {dff} = {dff//d}*d_model")
    print(f"  attn == all linear terms at n = 2d + d_ff = {2*d + dff}")

def kv_cache_table(d=4096, H=32, L=32, n=8192, bytes_per=2):
    dh = d // H
    print(f"  d={d} H={H} d_head={dh} L={L} n={n}  fp16 KV cache, one sequence")
    for name, Hkv in (("MHA (H_kv=32)", 32), ("GQA (H_kv=8)", 8), ("GQA (H_kv=4)", 4),
                      ("MQA (H_kv=1)", 1)):

        print(f"  {name:<16} {b/2**30:8.3f} GiB   reduction {H/Hkv:5.1f}x")

The forward pass matches torch to $8.882\times10^{-16}$ in float64, the worst relative gradient error over four sampled coordinates per tensor is $6.474\times10^{-9}$, and cached decoding reproduces the full forward to $8.882\times10^{-16}$ — exactly, not approximately, because the arithmetic is identical, just reordered. The FLOP table crosses over at $n = 3072 = 4d$ for $d = 768$, with the attention/FFN ratio running $0.042$ at $n=128$, $1.000$ at $n=3072$, and $5.333$ at $n=16384$.

Under Encryption

Softmax is the hardest single operation in an encrypted transformer, and it is hard three times over. It needs an exponential, which is not a polynomial and must be approximated on a bounded range; a division by a data-dependent sum, which under CKKS means an iterative inverse (Goldschmidt or Newton) costing several multiplicative levels; and a maximum subtraction for numerical stability, which is a comparison — the single most expensive primitive in homomorphic arithmetic, since comparison is a step function and needs a deep composite-polynomial approximation. The matmuls on either side, $QK^{\top}$ and $AV$, are the one thing FHE does well.

This is why every system in Chapter 40 is structurally a different answer to the same question about softmax: approximate it polynomially, hand it to a garbled circuit or a secure two-party protocol, or abandon it. Some abandon it — replacing $\mathrm{softmax}(s)$ with a normalized $\mathrm{ReLU}(s)$ or a plain quadratic, both worse in the clear but evaluable under encryption at a fraction of the depth. Note that the mask is free: adding $-\infty$ (in practice, a large negative plaintext constant) to a ciphertext logit is a plaintext addition, depth zero. Chapter 38 does the depth arithmetic for one attention block, and Chapter 39 takes up the approximation problem in detail.

Key Takeaways
  • Attention is soft dictionary lookup: $\mathrm{softmax}(QK^{\top}/\sqrt{d_k})V$ produces a convex combination of value rows, so it can interpolate among stored values but never extrapolate beyond them. Certain
  • The $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ factor exists solely to keep logit variance at $\Theta(1)$ at initialization, because $\mathrm{Var}(q^{\top}k) = d_k$ and a saturated softmax has a vanishing Jacobian $\mathrm{diag}(p) - pp^{\top}$. Certain
  • Mask by adding $-\infty$ to logits, never by multiplying the probabilities: the second version leaves the softmax denominator wrong, trains anyway, and silently converges worse. Certain
  • A block costs $12d^2 + 13d$ parameters and $8nd^2 + 4nd\,d_{\text{ff}} + 4n^2d$ forward FLOPs; the quadratic term overtakes the FFN at $n = d_{\text{ff}} = 4d$ and all linear terms at $n = 6d$. Certain
  • GQA at $H_{kv} = 8$ cuts the KV cache $4\times$ (MQA, $32\times$) at little measured quality cost, but whether head-count reduction or low-rank latent caching is the right axis is unsettled and needs a matched-cache-byte comparison. Contested
